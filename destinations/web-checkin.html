Check in, the easy way

i.

Keep your PNR ready

The 6-character booking reference from your ticket email or SMS, along with the passenger's last name exactly as on the ticket.

ii.

Open at the right time

Most airlines open check-in 48 hours before departure (24 hours for many international flights) and close 60–75 minutes before.

iii.

Choose seats & add-ons

Pick a free seat or let the airline assign one. Add extra baggage or meals only if you need them.

iv.

Save your boarding pass

Download the PDF or save to your phone. Link it with DigiYatra at Indian airports for a faster entry.

Unaccompanied minors, passengers needing wheelchair assistance, and those travelling with pets usually have to check in at the airport counter. Check-in windows can change — always confirm on the airline's website before you fly.

What you can carry, and what you can't

A quick guide for air travel in India. Always match it against your airline's own rules.

✓ In your cabin bag

Hand baggage that goes on board with you

  • One cabin bag — usually up to 7 kg (some airlines 8 kg) and about 55 × 35 × 25 cm, plus one small personal item such as a handbag or laptop bag.
  • Power banks and spare lithium batteries — cabin only, never in checked baggage. Up to 100 Wh is fine; 100–160 Wh needs airline approval.
  • Phones, laptops, tablets, cameras and chargers.
  • Medicines in their original packs, with the prescription handy. Insulin and syringes need a doctor's note.
  • Baby food, milk and essentials for an infant travelling with you.
  • Liquids on international flights — containers of 100 ml or less, all together in one clear, resealable 1-litre bag.
  • Dry snacks, an empty water bottle (fill it after security), a shawl or jacket.
  • Valuables and documents — ID, tickets, cash, jewellery, and any medicine you cannot do without.

⚑ Checked bag only

Allowed in the hold, not in the cabin

  • Liquids over 100 ml — perfume, oils, lotions, large water bottles, sealed in leak-proof bottles.
  • Sharp items — knives, scissors, razor blades, nail cutters with blades, tools, trekking poles and ice axes.
  • Sports gear — cricket bats, golf clubs, hockey sticks, skates.
  • Dry coconut (copra) and ghee, pickles and oils in sealed packing.
  • Gangajal and sealed prasad liquids in tightly closed containers.
  • Pooja items with sharp or pointed parts — a trishul, a diya stand with a spike, a small knife for cutting fruit.
  • Trekking and camping kit without fuel — tent poles, a stove with no gas, a pocket knife.

✕ Not allowed at all

Not in your cabin bag, and not in your checked bag

  • Explosives and fireworks — crackers, sparklers, gunpowder, flares.
  • Flammable liquids and gases — petrol, lighter fluid, spirit, paint thinner, gas cylinders, aerosol sprays above the permitted size.
  • Lighters and matchboxes — generally not permitted in hand baggage in India.
  • Camphor (kapoor) and loose incense powders that burn easily.
  • Firearms, ammunition and replicas unless you hold a valid licence and the airline has approved them in advance.
  • Corrosives, poisons and toxic chemicals — acid, bleach, mercury, pesticides.
  • Damaged or recalled batteries, and spare lithium batteries in the checked bag.
  • Illegal items — drugs, counterfeit goods, wildlife products.

Going on a yatra? Packing tips

  • Prasad and sweets are fine in the cabin when dry and sealed; carry wet or oily prasad in the checked bag.
  • Flowers and tulsi malas are allowed. Fresh garlands can wilt; pack them flat.
  • Dry coconut must go in the checked bag — it is banned from hand baggage.
  • Gangajal is best packed in a sealed can in the checked bag.
  • Copper and brass utensils are fine; a heavy kalash is better in the checked bag.
  • Pill boxes and medicines stay in your cabin bag so you always have them.
  • Carry a photo ID that matches the name on your ticket — Aadhaar, passport, driving licence or voter ID.
  • Arrive early — 2 hours for domestic and 3 hours for international flights leaves time for security.
This is general guidance based on Indian aviation security rules (BCAS and DGCA) and international standards (ICAO). Baggage limits, fees and the items allowed differ by airline, route and fare, and security officers can refuse any item. Check your ticket and the airline's baggage page before you pack.
